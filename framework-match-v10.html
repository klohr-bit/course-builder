The Framework Match

Find the design approach that fits what you're actually teaching.

Five questions about the shape of your expertise. The result is a real recommendation, grounded in instructional design research, with three first pieces drafted for your specific context.

About 4 minutes

You don't need to know what you want to build yet. Most people who do this don't.

01 of 06

When someone has truly learned this from you, how would you know?

Don't worry if more than one feels true. Pick the closest.

Try this

Think of one person you've taught. Informally counts. When you knew they'd gotten it, what were you actually noticing? A specific thing they did, or how they were thinking?

02 of 06

How much do the situations vary, in your work?

Same situation every time, or no two ever quite the same?

Try this

When you're doing this work, how often are you adapting on the fly versus running a familiar play?

03 of 06

How much of what you know lives in your head versus on paper?

Most people overestimate how much of it they could write down. Be honest.

Try this

If a smart, capable person read your best blog post or watched your best video, could they go do the work? Or would they still be missing something that's hard to put words to?

04 of 06

Is what you do one focused thing, or several things working together?

It's fine if the answer surprises you.

Try this

If you taught someone every individual piece of what you do, perfectly, would they be able to do the whole job? Or is something else needed?

05 of 06

What happens when someone gets it wrong?

A bruised ego, a lost client, a lawsuit. These aren't the same thing.

Try this

When someone you've taught makes a mistake doing this work, what's typically the worst thing that happens?

06 of 06

Last bit. Tell me about your work.

Don't overthink it. A few sentences about what you do, and who you do it for.

The more specific you are about your actual work, the better the result.

I'm not sure what I'd teach yet, show me the alternative.
That's the most common starting point

Most people who arrive here haven't decided yet. Just describe the work you do. What you're known for, what people come to you for, what you'd be sad to stop doing. The diagnostic still works.

What feels right for now?

Don't overthink this either. You can change direction later, this just shapes the language of your result.
